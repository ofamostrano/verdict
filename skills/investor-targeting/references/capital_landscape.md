# Capital Landscape

A standing map of capital sources, oriented to a Boston-based founder building software with an eventual national market. Firm strategies drift, funds close, and partners move; treat every entry as a starting hypothesis to verify before outreach rather than a current fact. Verify stage focus, active check size, and portfolio conflicts at the time the list is built.

## How to use this file

Work outward from the innermost circle. Geographic proximity plus thesis fit produces the highest reply rate for a first-time founder, and local firms are reachable through local events, university networks, and portfolio founders in ways that national firms are not. Move outward only as evidence accumulates.

These firms lead seed rounds selectively and lead Series A onward routinely. Approach with the evidence bar met.

### Local angel groups

Formal screening then presentation cycles running weeks; start early and expect a process rather than a meeting. These groups fund the range below institutional pre-seed and frequently syndicate with each other.

Notable groups include LaunchPad Venture Group, Boston Harbor Angels, Beacon Angels, Walnut Ventures, Hub Angels, and the regional Keiretsu Forum chapter. Golden Seeds invests nationally in women-led companies and is relevant where the founding team qualifies.

### University-affiliated funds

Eligibility is usually tied to an affiliation; verify before pursuing.

### Local life sciences and healthcare specialists

Dominant in the Boston ecosystem and worth naming for accuracy, but they fund therapeutics and platform biology rather than vertical software. Do not target them for a software MVP unless the product is genuinely clinical or research infrastructure. Principal names: Atlas Venture, Third Rock Ventures, RA Capital Management, SV Health Investors.

## Tier 2 — National pre-seed, seed, and accelerators

The correct outward move once local conversations are underway. Many of these firms invest remotely and run standardized processes.

**Accelerators and programs.** Y Combinator and Techstars are the two largest and both run fixed batch cycles, so work backward from the deadline. Techstars operates location- and vertical-specific programs, several of which have Boston relevance. Forum Ventures runs a B2B SaaS-focused program with an investment component.

**Institutional pre-seed and seed funds.** SV Angel, BoxGroup, First Round Capital, NFX, Global Founders Capital, Speedinvest, 500 Global, Redbud VC, and Slauson & Co all participate at pre-seed or seed. Right Side Capital Management is distinctive for writing very small checks at high volume with an explicitly quantitative process, which makes it a reasonable early cold target where the metrics are clean.

**Syndicates and angel networks.** AngelList syndicates aggregate individual checks behind a lead and can fill a round quickly once a lead exists. Tech Coast Angels and New York Angels are large regional groups that consider companies outside their metros. Keiretsu Forum operates chapters nationally.

Two notes on sequencing. A syndicate generally requires a lead investor and a set price before it is useful, so approach syndicates after a lead is in hand rather than before. Accelerator deadlines are immovable and should anchor the outreach calendar.

## Tier 3 — Multistage and growth

Relevant as the named next round rather than as a first raise. Naming the intended Series A firms in a seed conversation demonstrates a plan; approaching them without Series A metrics wastes the introduction.

Principal names: Andreessen Horowitz, Insight Partners, Sequoia Capital, Tiger Global, Founders Fund, Thrive Capital, Kleiner Perkins, Khosla Ventures, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Accel, New Enterprise Associates, and General Catalyst at its later-stage practice.

Where a firm is subject to material public legal or regulatory proceedings, note it as a practical consideration in sequencing and diligence expectations rather than as a judgment on the firm.

## Non-dilutive: Massachusetts and federal

Run in parallel with equity outreach. An awarded grant is validation that costs no equity, and several of these programs explicitly fund the pre-revenue stage where equity investors will not.

### Massachusetts and regional

| Program | Nature |
|---|---|
| MassVentures START | Non-dilutive grants for Massachusetts companies commercializing SBIR-funded technology |
| MassVentures early-stage investment | State-affiliated venture capital for Massachusetts companies |
| Massachusetts Life Sciences Center | Grants, loans, and tax incentives for life-sciences companies with Massachusetts operations |
| MassCEC | Clean-energy and climate-technology funding and programs |
| Massachusetts Technology Collaborative | Technology-sector programs and initiatives |
| Municipal and regional economic development offices | Small grants, space, and hiring incentives, often overlooked and low-competition |

### Federal

SBIR and STTR are the principal vehicles, administered separately by participating agencies including NSF, NIH, the Department of Energy, the Department of Defense, and others. Each agency runs its own solicitation calendar, topic list, and review criteria. Phase I awards fund feasibility; Phase II funds development. These programs fund technical and research risk rather than commercial traction, which makes them accessible before the equity evidence bar is met.

Practical guidance. Read the specific solicitation rather than general program descriptions, since topics and criteria change per cycle. Write to the published review criteria in the program's own language. Note that agency review cycles typically run several months, so the application calendar must be planned well ahead of the cash need. And confirm the call is open before recommending it; a closed solicitation is not a pathway.

### Other non-dilutive sources

Sector-specific foundations and challenge prizes exist in health, climate, education, and civic technology. Corporate cloud and platform credit programs reduce burn materially at early stage without being capital at all, and several are available to any startup on application.

## Verification checklist before delivering a target list

Confirm each firm still invests at the intended stage and check size, using recent announced deals rather than the firm's own historical positioning. Check the portfolio for direct competitors. Identify the specific partner whose stated interests match, since firms do not make decisions but partners do. Confirm any accelerator or grant deadline against the program's current page. Finally, confirm the geography assumption: several firms formerly anchored to one metro now invest remotely, and several nominally national firms in practice concentrate near their offices.
